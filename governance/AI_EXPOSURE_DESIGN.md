## 1. Construct split

"Occupational AI exposure" names at least three different constructs. A menu that mixes them produces disagreement that is a category error rather than measurement noise.

**C1: direct technical feasibility (task level).** Could a current AI system perform, or substantially speed up, this task at acceptable quality, regardless of whether anyone uses it that way? The unit is the O\*NET task (or DWA). The occupation score is a declared aggregation over the occupation's tasks. C1 is a property of the technology–task pair. Rubric ratings (Eloundou et al.), ability–application relatedness (AIOE), patent–task text overlap (Webb) and ML-suitability rubrics (SML) are all candidate measurement functions for C1, and they disagree.

**C2: observed adoption (task level), split into C2a automation and C2b augmentation.** What share of real AI usage maps to this task? **C2a** covers usage in which the AI performs the task (directive and feedback-loop patterns in the Anthropic Economic Index; the "AI action" side in Tomlinson et al.). **C2b** covers usage in which a human performs the task with AI help (task-iteration, learning and validation patterns; the "user goal" side). C2 is a property of platform users × tasks. It mixes feasibility with demand, platform selection, firm policy and regulation.

**C3: systemic or network exposure.** This is exposure an occupation receives through its position in labor-market networks (mobility flows, skill similarity, career ladders), as distinct from its own tasks. An occupation with low direct exposure can still be exposed through its neighbours: entry roles that feed an exposed occupation, or fallback occupations that absorb displaced workers. The unit is the occupation, embedded in a network W.

**Why C1 is the flagship.** C1 has the largest published menu, with several independent construction methods. It also has the documented disagreement this package exists to discipline: an ~11× spread and a sign flip (Ogut & Yin 2026), and the cognitive-content relabeling critique (Rai 2026). C1 is also the measure applied labor work actually puts in regressions. C2 is a better *anchor* than menu: it sits on a different method axis from C1 (Campbell–Fiske), and its available forms are proprietary-platform aggregates. **Why C3 is the extension.** C3 takes C1 survivors as inputs, so it cannot be defined until C1's admissible set is frozen. It is also where Augusto's complexity-science interest gives the package a differentiator: network propagation run as a menu, with a theory-stated screen. No prior-art search was done for this idea, so novelty must be checked before it is claimed.

**Open-weight arm protocol.** The arm follows the paper's Appendix "Open-weight LLM measurement protocol" (`paper/position_paper.tex`, `app:llm`). The prompt template, model id, pinned artifact hash, runtime/quantization/tokenizer, decoding, answer-token map and scalarization are all part of *m*, and each is recorded on the adapter card (Table `tab:adapter`). The paper lists **prompt paraphrase as remaining work** (l. 863), so paraphrase variants extend the appendix rather than reproduce it. The rationale is instability evidence: Yin, Vu & Persico (NBER w35110, April 2026) report a 3.6-fold divergence in mean exposure across three frontier models and model agreement as low as 57%. **Owner choices:** (a) whether each (model, paraphrase) pair is its own measure or paraphrases are averaged per model; (b) the open-weight model families; (c) whether a "positive control" exists. No accepted C1 ground truth exists, and that absence is a design fact to state in the paper rather than paper over.

**Rai (2026) verification, before relying on it.** The paper is MPRA 129904, deposited 2026-07-08: a single-author working paper, not peer-reviewed. Its PDF was read 2026-10-20 and the numbers above match the text. **Four caveats.** First, its "Eloundou" column is `human_rating_beta`, not GPT-4 β, so the GPT-4 column is untested by Rai. Second, its cognitive index is built from O\*NET abilities, the same input family AIOE uses, which makes the AIOE result partly mechanical. That is why the proposal draws the discriminant from DOT. Third, its results are unweighted (an employment-weighted check is reported as similar). Fourth, its RTI is an O\*NET re-construction, not Autor–Dorn's DOT original. Status: **verified as reported; not replicated.**

**Known-groups caveat.** The `kg_high`/`kg_low` contrast lies on the same cognitive-vs-manual axis that `disc_cog` caps, so a pure cognitive-content measure passes both trivially. Known groups therefore cannot separate C1 from cognitive content; `disc_cog` does that job. An optional `kg_cogmatched` group (high-cognitive, low-feasibility occupations, e.g. surgeons, dentists, airline pilots) is listed as a **proposal without anchors**. Groups must come from non-menu evidence, because choosing them from menu outputs would be circular.

**Platform selection bias (convergent anchors).** Each platform's usage reflects its own user base: Claude.ai consumer traffic (API traffic added in later AEI releases), and US Bing Copilot between Jan and Sep 2024 (≈200k conversations). It does not reflect each occupation's workforce. Usage also mixes feasibility with demand and access. Two platforms give a heteromethod check, but they share selection: chat interfaces, desk work and English. Correlated bias is therefore reduced, not removed. **Do not use** AEI `labor_market_impacts/job_exposure.csv` (`observed_exposure`) as an anchor. Its construction was not verified here, and if it folds in a feasibility rating it shares inputs with the menu. **Pre-freeze coherence check (aux-only; never touches menu scores):** compute corr(AEI, Copilot), corr(anchors, `disc_cog` aux) and corr(anchors, `disc_rti` aux). If an anchor itself breaches the discriminant it asks measures to respect, the network is strained before any measure is scored (compare the trust-cell lesson: `gps_trust_cell` failed its own gate). Augusto decides before the freeze, never after.

**Anchor sources (verified 2026-10-20).** *AEI:* `huggingface.co/datasets/Anthropic/EconomicIndex`. The README says "Data released under CC-BY, code released under MIT License", while the HF metadata tag says `mit`; the README is the more specific statement. Releases run from 2025-02-10 to 2026-06-26, with task-level shares (`task_pct_v1/v2.csv`) and task-level collaboration patterns (`release_2025_03_27/automation_vs_augmentation_by_task.csv`). Tasks are keyed by **task text, not O\*NET task ID**, and the O\*NET version is UNVERIFIED. *Copilot:* Tomlinson, Jaffe, Wang, Counts & Suri, arXiv 2507.07935 v6 (2025-12-22, retitled "…Measuring the Applicability of Generative AI to Occupations"). Data: github.com/microsoft/working-with-ai v1.1, **CC BY 4.0** (LICENSE.txt). It uses O\*NET 29.0 IWAs, 2018 SOC, and BLS OEWS May 2023. Task weights are 2^importance × relevance, and IWAs are included only above a 0.05% activity share. Conversations are not released.

## 4. β candidates (proposal)

**β1 (primary): public "canaries" analogue.** The outcome per occupation *o* is y_o = Δlog E(22–25) − Δlog E(reference age, proposal 35–49), from IPUMS ACS 1-year microdata (`PERWT`). Proposed windows: pre = 2021–2022 pooled, post = 2024–2025 pooled. Skip 2020, which has only experimental ACS weights. β = `ols_coef`(y ~ z(m)). Proposed controls: a pre-period trend in y, and college share. CPS (monthly, IPUMS) is a secondary arm. **Reference result:** Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (revised 2026-08-12; ADP data through June 2026). In that paper, employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations is **19% below** the less-exposed counterfactual. In levels, the two most-exposed Eloundou GPT-4 β quintiles fell about 11% and the three least-exposed grew about 10% (Nov 2022–Jun 2026). Their main exposure measures are Eloundou GPT-4 β and AEI (March 2025 release), merged through a BLS 2010→2018 SOC crosswalk. **Their own public-data check is the main β1 risk:** over 2022–2024 the Q1–Q5 gap is **−0.022 [−0.055, +0.011] in the ACS versus −0.132 in ADP**. The two sources agree within professional, information and financial services (−0.231 ADP vs −0.213 ACS), and they report the CPS is noisy in fine age×occupation cells. A near-zero β1 range is therefore a plausible, valid finding. It does not mean the design failed.

**β2: the Ogut–Yin post-2022 employment coefficient.** Ogut & Yin, arXiv 2607.12219 v1 (2026-07-13), match six exposure measures to an ACS panel (2015–2024; 8.88M person-years). They retain five; an ex-ante factor-analytic rule separates Webb as a distinct construct. Their result is a loading-invariant consensus coefficient of **−0.239**, a partial-identification half-width of 1.23% of the point estimate, Imbens–Manski CIs with the Stoye critical value, a "factor of eleven" spread across scores, and a post-2022 sign change between the language-model measures and Webb. **Fit note:** their person-level regression has to be reduced upstream to an occupation-level outcome before it enters the engine. That reduction approximates their β; it is not a replication.

**How this design differs from Ogut–Yin.** Their screen is **menu-internal**: it uses the factor structure among the measures themselves, then assumes the consensus measurement function is linear and bounds curvature heterogeneity. The result is a tight interval around a cross-source estimator. This design screens on **external, theory-stated restrictions** using auxiliaries held out of the menu (two usage platforms, DOT task content, known groups). It adds a units-split holdout and makes no consensus-linearity assumption. The range is the image of β on survivors only, and it can be wide, include zero, or be empty (M\* = ∅ is an exit-0 result). Both designs treat Webb as distinct: theirs drops it by factor structure, while this one *tests* it against usage (prediction P1). Note for the paper: `position_paper.tex` l. 298 groups Ogut–Yin under work "without a surviving set". Because they retain five of six measures after a screen, that phrasing needs Augusto's review.

## 6. C3 extension sketch (proposal; after a C1 freeze)

**Menu.** m_C3[s, W, k](o) = Σ_{j≠o} W̃_oj · s(j). Here s ranges over the **frozen C1 survivors**, W̃ is row-normalized with no self-loops, and the propagation depth k ∈ {1, 2} is itself menu variation. The candidate networks W are:
- **CPS occupational transitions:** IPUMS CPS monthly panel flows, pre-2020 window.
- **O\*NET skill similarity:** cosine similarity on Skills/Knowledge importance vectors (O\*NET CC BY 4.0).
- **del Rio-Chanona, Mealy, Beguerisse-Díaz, Lafond & Farmer** (2021), "Occupational mobility and automation: a data-driven network model," *J. R. Soc. Interface* 18(174):20200898. This is a 464-node network on ACS 4-digit occupation codes, built from IPUMS CPS transitions 2010–2017 (construction from Mealy et al. 2018). Code and data are on Zenodo record 4453162 v2 (CC BY 4.0; the paper cites record 4305783).

Each (s, W, k) triple is a distinct measurement function.

**Restriction.** C3 should predict **entry-level hiring declines beyond direct exposure**. Theory anchor: in del Rio-Chanona et al., network structure alone raises steady-state unemployment from 4.1% (complete network) to 5.3%. Occupations with similar automation probability also diverge in long-run unemployment: dispatchers +21%, pharmacy aides −17%. **Lock conflict to resolve first:** R may not reference β's outcome (§11 D4 is aux-only, and the D5 outcome is β-only, never in R). The hiring auxiliary must therefore differ from β1's outcome in source or window, e.g. CPS 2023–2024 flows into occupation for ages 22–25. "Beyond direct exposure" also needs a partial association, and the registry has **no partial-correlation evaluator**. Option (i): residualize the auxiliary upstream on one frozen direct-exposure reference column. Option (ii): a dated decision plus a fixture for a new evaluator, which would be an engine change outside this container. **Discriminant:** a `corr_zero` ceiling between m_C3 and its own seed s(o), so that homophily (neighbors resemble you) does not pass as network exposure.

## 7. Risks

- **Data licenses.** AIOE has no license file. Webb is behind an email sign-up with no stated terms. SML's openICPSR terms are unverified. Eloundou is MIT; Copilot data, AEI data, O\*NET and the del Rio-Chanona data are CC BY (4.0 where stated). **IPUMS forbids redistributing microdata**, so only derived occupation-level aggregates can enter the repo, and those need a disclosure check. Proprietary-origin anchors need an explicit D6 reading (Authority, flag ii).
- **SOC crosswalk.** Native vintages differ:

  | Source | Native codes |
  |---|---|
  | Eloundou | O\*NET-SOC 2019 (8-digit, O\*NET 27.2) |
  | Tomlinson | SOC 2018 |
  | AEI | O\*NET task text |
  | AIOE | 6-digit SOC, vintage unverified |
  | Webb, SML | unverified |
  | Dorn RTI | occ1990dd, crosswalks only to 2010 Census codes |
  | del Rio-Chanona | ACS 4-digit, 2010-era |
  | ACS | 2018 Census occupation codes from 2018 on |

  Many-to-many splits and their weights are part of *m* and enter the freeze. Canaries reports that its improved crosswalk moved firm-fixed-effects estimates. Propose a single frozen crosswalk bundle, sha256-pinned, shared by every measure.
- **Small cells.** Age 22–25 × detailed occupation in a 1-year ACS (about a 1% sample) leaves many cells thin, and Canaries found CPS fine cells "noisy". Proposal: a respondent floor (the §11 analogue is ≥30), pooled years and coarser units for thin occupations. All three are freeze-config choices, never post-hoc. The new disclosure-avoidance order may also coarsen or perturb PUMS occupation detail. Check this when the 2025 PUMS lands.
- **Goodhart after publication.** Once R is public, new exposure measures can be tuned to the AEI/Copilot anchors, and the anchors become targets. Proposal: the adapter-card *Provenance* field records whether a measure was built after the network's publication date, mirroring `pre_data` anchors. Post-publication measures are flagged, never silently mixed into a confirmatory run.
- **Anchor drift.** AEI releases revise over time, and Canaries pools several. Pin one release by sha256 and treat a later release as a new auxiliary, which means a new `network_hash`.
